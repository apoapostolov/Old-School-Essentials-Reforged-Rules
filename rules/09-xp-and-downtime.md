# XP and Downtime

## Level Up

### XP and Economy

This adventure uses an x10 faster leveling speed than old-school D&D to allow for several level-ups during a single adventure and the ability to progress into later tiers of the game within modern day time constraints. The following changes apply:

- Characters earn 1xp for each 1sp (not 1gp!) of treasure secured during adventuring. Although the world economy and other factors may prevent them from converting all treasure into its nominal gold price, they will earn XP based on its nominal price.

### After Level 9

Upon gaining a new level past level 9, you may reroll your hit dice (typically limited to 9) and apply the new value to your maximum hit points if it is higher than your previous rolled value.

## Downtimes

### Life Time

- Each day of life outside of adventuring since the start of the adventuring career earns 1 XP. This includes a bit of training, occasional altercations, hardships, and events that happen to the character and their family. This experience is usually a reward for the player for surviving the adventure, based on the time between the previous and the next adventure; or a little bit of progress if the DM needs to move the timeline in the future but the character has not leveled up at all meanwhile.
- For every three levels of experience earned by the character, the gain of experience from such life events increases by 1 XP per day.

### Learn New Spells

Any class that can learn and cast spells gains XP when they learn a new spell they did not know already from sources such as discovered scrolls and spell books found while adventuring; spells received from other players do not provide XP. The XP earned is based on the type of spell learned:

- Common spells from the Old School Essentials Player's Tome provide XP equal to the spell's level multiplied by 50.
- Rare and unique spells from other sources, such as published supplements or created by the DM for the adventure, provide XP equal to the spell's level multiplied by 150, or more if the DM deems the spell extremely powerful or rare.

### Philanthropy

While residing in a civilized settlement, a PC may donate gold to philanthropic causes to gain experience points and social standing. Characters receive XP equal to 80% of gold donated. Donations can be made as frequently as desired-donate 10 gp today, 50 gp next week, 200 gp before leaving town. Track cumulative donations for social consequence purposes.

__Philanthropic Acts:__ Donations can take many forms-funding temple reconstruction, sponsoring orphanages, providing grain during famine, financing public works, patronizing the arts, or supporting local militias. The DM may adjust consequences based on the nature and appropriateness of the donation.

__Social Consequences:__ Once your cumulative donations to a settlement reach a threshold, roll 2d6 and consult the table below. Add +1 if donations align with your character's stated values or class purpose (e.g., Clerics donating to temples, Paladins funding the poor). Add +1 for the Generous threshold or +2 for the Legendary threshold.

#### Social Thresholds

| __Settlement Size__ | __Modest (2d6)__ | __Notable (2d6)__ | __Generous (2d6+1)__ | __Legendary (2d6+2)__ |
| ------------------- | ---------------- | ----------------- | -------------------- | --------------------- |
| Village             | 100 gp           | 300 gp            | 600 gp               | 1000 gp               |
| Town                | 200 gp           | 500 gp            | 1000 gp              | 2000 gp               |
| City                | 400 gp           | 1000 gp           | 2000 gp              | 4000 gp               |
| Metropolis          | 800 gp           | 2000 gp           | 4000 gp              | 8000 gp               |

When you cross a threshold for the first time, roll 2d6 once on the consequences table below (applying threshold and alignment bonuses). The result is permanent for this settlement. You only roll once per threshold-crossing Modest gives one roll, crossing Notable later gives another roll, etc. The Legendary threshold can be repeated-every time you donate an additional threshold amount (1000 gp in a village, 2000 gp in a town, etc.), you may roll again on the consequence table.

#### Philanthropic Consequences (2d6)

| __Roll__ | __Result__                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 2        | __Outrage:__ The locals see your donation as an insult or bribe. -2 to Reaction rolls in this settlement. You may not benefit from philanthropy here again until you complete a dangerous quest that directly aids the community. Local authorities become suspicious.                      |
| 3        | __Resentment:__ Your wealth is seen as ill-gotten or your charity condescending. -1 to Reaction rolls in this settlement. You cannot gain XP from philanthropy here until you perform meaningful community service (DM's discretion).                                                       |
| 4        | __Mistrust:__ People question where your gold came from. -1 to Reaction rolls with merchants and guards. Rumors spread about your past.                                                                                                                                                     |
| 5        | __Skepticism:__ The donation is accepted but your motives are doubted. No mechanical effect, but NPCs remain wary. Future Reaction rolls in edge cases may suffer -1.                                                                                                                       |

__Alignment Considerations:__ Lawful characters gain an additional +1 to the consequence roll when donating to established institutions (temples, guilds, governments). Chaotic characters gain +1 when their donation disrupts the status quo or aids the downtrodden directly.

__Limits:__ Track cumulative donations per settlement. Thresholds are permanent-once you've rolled for a threshold, you can't roll again for that threshold in this settlement. Negative consequences can be overcome through direct community service as determined by the DM.

### Carousing

While residing in a settlement, a PC may spend gold on feasting, drinking, and socializing to build personal relationships and gather information. Carousing does not grant XP-you're buying experiences and connections, not training. Specify how much you spend and where you carouse, then roll 2d6 on the table below.

__Venue and Spending:__ Choose a venue and roll for the cost. If you don't have enough gold to cover the roll, you run a tab-you owe the difference and must repay it before you can carouse at this venue again. The DM determines available venues based on settlement size and character social standing.

| __Venue__        | __Cost Roll__ | __Who You Meet and What you Get__                  |
| ---------------- | ------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Common Alehouse  | 1d8+1 gp      | Laborers, farmers, local gossip                    |
| Soldier's Tavern | 2d8+8 gp      | Mercenaries, guards, war veterans                  |
| Criminal Den     | 2d10+5 gp     | Thieves, smugglers, black market contacts          |
| Brothel          | 3d8+10 gp     | Courtesans, spies, scandalous secrets              |
| Guild Hall       | 5d10+20 gp    | Craftsmen, merchants, trade secrets                |
| Gambling Haven   | 5d12+25 gp    | High-stakes players, crime bosses, dangerous debts |
| Noble Court      | 10d10+50 gp   | Knights, nobles, political intrigue                |

__Bonus Spending:__ After rolling the venue cost, you may voluntarily spend additional gold. Add +1 to the carousing outcome roll for every 50 gp spent beyond the rolled cost (maximum +3 bonus).

#### Complications (1d6)

Roll when instructed by the outcome table. The DM chooses or rolls:

| __1d10__ | __Complication__                                                                                                               |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1        | __Brawl:__ Started a fight-minor injuries, fines, or new enemies                                                               |
| 2        | __Gambling Loss:__ Lost money gambling-lose amount up to half the gold you spent carousing                                     |
| 3        | __Romantic Entanglement:__ Wanted or unwanted attention with complications, or rumors spread                                   |
| 4        | __Loose Lips:__ You said something you shouldn't have-someone might have overheard, or maybe they didn't                       |
| 5        | __Challenge:__ Challenged to a duel, contest, or bet you can't easily refuse                                                   |
| 6        | __Made an Enemy:__ You gravely insulted or wronged someone who now actively works against you-they remember, even if you don't |
| 7        | __Theft:__ Something valuable was stolen from you-coin purse, equipment, or a personal item                                    |
| 8        | __Compromised:__ Someone saw or learned something about you they could use as leverage or blackmail                            |
| 9        | __Obligation:__ You agreed to do something while drunk-an oath, a promise, or a contract you can't easily break                |
| 10       | __Hunted:__ You attracted attention from authorities, bounty hunters, or assassins-someone now tracks you                      |

#### Opportunities (1d10)

Roll when instructed by the outcome table. The DM chooses or rolls:

| __1d10__ | __Opportunity__                                                                                                      |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1        | __Hiring Offer:__ Someone offers you paid work, a quest, or a business venture-or someone is willing to work for you |
| 2        | __Rare Goods:__ You learn where to acquire hard-to-find items at a fair price                                        |
| 3        | __Inside Information:__ You overhear a valuable secret about a rival or threat                                       |
| 4        | __Patronage:__ Someone wealthy offers sponsorship or a letter of introduction                                        |
| 5        | __Safe Haven:__ You gain access to a private space-a room, workshop, or hideout                                      |
| 6        | __Alliance Opportunity:__ A faction invites you to join or assist them                                               |
| 7        | __Trade Route:__ You learn of a profitable trade opportunity or resource location worth investigating                |
| 8        | __Heirloom Gift:__ Someone gives you a valuable item-weapon, jewelry, or magic item-out of gratitude or respect      |
| 9        | __Legal Protection:__ You gain temporary immunity, a writ of safe passage, or legal favor from authorities           |
| 10       | __Secret Society:__ You're invited to join an exclusive organization with hidden resources and influence             |

__Faction Alignment:__ Carousing with one group may alienate another. Drinking with the city watch makes thieves wary. Feasting with one noble house insults their rivals. The DM decides when carousing creates factional consequences.

__Maintaining Contacts:_* Relationships from carousing fade without upkeep. If you haven't caroused with a contact in three months of game time, the DM may reduce their loyalty or willingness to help. Philanthropy builds monuments; carousing builds friendships that require maintenance.
