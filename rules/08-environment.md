# Environment

## Environment

### Darkness

Attacking a creature you cannot see imposes penalties based on lighting conditions and available sensory information:

*_Blinded, or Pitch Darkness (no light source):__ -4 to attack rolls, and all complex interactions require a check at -4 darkness penalty. Creatures rely entirely on sound, air flow and smell, and touch to locate targets. Assassins and Thieves may use pitch darkness as a substitute for surprise, as long as their exact location cannot be pinpointed by their target by other senses like sound or smell, and they succeed on Move Silent check rolled before any movement or action. Creatures with infravision ignore these penalties in pitch darkness but suffer the standard penalties if any light source is present within their range.

__Moonlit Darkness or Dim Light (edges of torchlight):__ -2 to attack rolls. Creatures can perceive vague shapes and movement but lack detail for precise strikes. This applies at the edge of a torch's 30' radius or in open moonlight with partial concealment. Creatures with low-light vision ignore these penalties in moonlit darkness and dim light.

__Other Senses:__ A character with exceptional sense of smell (such as animal companions) or hearing (character with Hear Noise 4-in-6 or better) reduce darkness penalties by 1 for each exceptional sense.

### Torches

The following torches are available for sale in most villages:

__Standard Torch (1 sp, 1 cn):__ Pitch-coated wood bundle. Burns 1d4+4 turns (5-8 turns), produces 30' light radius. Produces smell of tar and smoke, will sputter in rain and wind environments, with a risk of 2-in-6 to extinguish in extreme gust or heavy rain.

__Birch-Bark Torch (2 sp, 1 cn):__ Dried birch bark roll. Burns 1d6+5 turns (6-11 turns), produces 40' light radius. Burns cleaner with minimal smoke; performs better in gusts and damp conditions (upgrades chance to extinguish to 1-in-6).

__Pine-Resin Torch (4 sp, 2 cn):__ Resin-packed wooden core. Burns 2d4+6 turns (8-14 turns), produces 50' light radius. Produces heavy smoke and pungent aroma, both which can be traced to use of torches in a closed air environment. Extremely flammable; cannot be easily extinguished. Reserved for extended and military expeditions. The lingering smell makes them a liability during stealth missions.

The DM rolls this duration privately and tracks the countdown without revealing it to the party. When a torch has 2 turns remaining, the DM announces: "Your torch is guttering. The flame flickers and weakens." When the turn count expires, the torch extinguishes and complete darkness descends unless another light source is lit.

A character holding a torch occupies one hand with it. Torches mounted in wall sconces or affixed to gear do not occupy a hand but cannot be moved. A character may deliberately extinguish a torch to conserve it. Relighting a torch requires flint and steel and takes 1 minute; lighting a torch in pitch darkness is a Dexterity check at standard -4 darkness penalty. Spells or magical effects relight torches instantly.

A typical adventurer carries 3-6 torches at any time. Hiring a Torchbearer (1 sp/day, retainer) preserves hand economy and ensures backup light. Multiple party members carrying torches provide redundancy in case one is lost or extinguished.

### Infravision (a.k.a. Old-School Darkvision)

Infravision only works in pitch darkness. If a creature with infravision is within range of a light source, the infravision is disabled until the creature is no longer within the range of a light source and not facing any light source.

Infravision only detects blotches of heat without any actual form, and rough silhouettes of environments where heat is present. They cannot read or notice fine details with infravision, such as recognizing monsters except by size, silhouette (differentiating two and four-legged form, or the difference between an orc and a goblin), and behavior (how they move). They cannot recognize any fine detail or visual properties such as color of individual objects.

### Low Light Vision

Creatures with infravision also have low-light vision. Creatures with low light vision see twice as far in torchlight, up to 60 feet rather than 30 feet.

### Moonlit Vision

In an open exterior where moonlight is present, if the moon cycle matches half-moon, three-quarters or full moon, the environment is considered dim light unless there is concealment (medium to thick woods, buildings casting shadows, etc.). In moonlit darkness, everyone gains Low Light Vision after 2 turns of scotopic adaptation, while creatures with low light vision see as if it were normal light (with caveats). Exposure to a bright source of light for 1 turn resets the scotopic adaptation in creatures without low light vision.
