# General Rules

## Rolling Dice

Any specific X-in-6 check and Skill Check (d20 under one of the six scores, or a class feature percentile check) is rolled only once per encounter, and for the whole party. There is no "individual checks" where the chance of success is multiplied by the number of individuals in the party, unless one person failing an individual check may fail the whole group, as decided by the DM.

Normally every character has a 1-in-6 chance to succeed at any common activity that doesn't require a skill check or a trained proficiency; such as listening or searching. Once a X-in-6 check is made, it cannot be repeated by another character. The rolled result represent the luck of the moment, not the luck of the individual. On a failure, compare the value across all characters not distracted with another activity. Another character may succeed using the rolled value that a character has failed with (such as rolling 2 for Listen check may be failure for a human, but a success for an Elf). If the value is 1 and everyone would succeed, the person who showed narrative initiative is the first to succeed. If the value is 2 or more and one or more other characters would succeed, the other characters succeed and the information is first revealed to them.

Where the outcome of a X-in-6 roll is unknown (such as listening, or searching for hidden treasure or wall passages) the roll is always made by the DM in secret. The party may spend additional time (typically a minimum of 2 extra turns, 2 extra hours, or an extra half day) to repeat the activity as a group. If they do, the chance of the party increases from the base X-in-6 by +1, +2 or even +3 for each time increase. This may allow the party to succeed against the rolled value, held in secret by the DM, but the party can never know for certain. The DM reveals the success once enough time has been committed to the activity to match the rolled result.

The same principles are applied to "roll under score" skill checks that use skills and training proficiencies, or a roll of a percentile die for class heroic skills. If a character asks to make a skill check rolling d20 under one of the six basic scores, or roll a d100 percentile dice, the rolled value becomes the luck of the moment. Even if they fail, another character may succeed if their base score is equal or above the rolled value, and they share the same training proficiencies. Typically the party should investigate any opportunities to use circumstances and tools to get bonuses to their scores before rolling the die in order to increase their chances.

> 👁️ If the Party fails a 1-in-6 check, any Thief in the party can treat their class feature percentile skill as a second chance. This check is once again “a luck of the moment” and the value is shared between multiple thieves in the party, where one with a higher level may succeed over one of lower level.

## Grogs

Any player character who is a purely a spellcaster (Cleric, Druid, Illusionist, and the Magic User; the DM may approve one for the Gnome as well) may choose to start their career with a grog, a loyal starting hireling sworn to assist and protect them during their early days of adventuring. The grog is typically a 1 HD Level 0 Fighter (or equivalent class-appropriate hireling, such as an animal companion for Druids/Rangers or an apprentice for pure spellcasters like Magic Users or Illusionists, subject to DM approval). The grog's hit dice is rolled normally as for L0 Class character (not maximized like leveled hirelings).

- __Bond & Role__: Players should establish a strong motivation and backstory bond between the PC and grog (e.g., family retainer, indebted mercenary, faithful hound). The grog prioritizes the PC's safety, carrying critical gear and acting as a dedicated bodyguard or utility hireling. The grog benefits from +2 Morale bonus due to sworn loyalty.
- __Economics__: No wage required, but the grog must be __treated fairly__ (adequate rations, rest, no suicidal orders), and failure risks Morale checks change from +2 to -2. The Grog receives a __half-share of treasure__ and earns XP from the hireling pool (the first 100 XP earned convert it to L1 hireling with maximized HP from its first class Hit Die).
- __You Only Get One Grog:__ If the grog dies, it is __not replaced.__ You must hire normally thereafter.

### Literacy in Karameikos

In the traditional society of the Grand Duchy of Karameikos, literacy remains a privilege largely confined to the educated elite. Most knowledge is preserved and transmitted orally through folklore, epic tales, songs, and family traditions. Practical literacy is commonplace only among merchants, tradesmen, administrators, and those involved in governance or trade with the Thyatian Empire. To determine a character’s literacy level, make a number of __Ability Checks under Intelligence__ equal to the value shown in the Family Social Standing table. Each successful check grants one Literacy Success.

__Special Adjustments:_*

- Barbarians and Druids receive *_one fewer check__ than indicated.
- Clerics, Magic-Users, and Illusionists automatically gain __one free Literacy Success__ (added after all checks are resolved). Magic-Users and Illusionists can choose to take Glantrian as their first literate language instead of Traladar. Most imported or spell-books used by travelling magic users prefer to use Glantrian so they aren’t widely used if stolen. Clerics use Monastic Script for the same reason, using so complex caligraphy that it is very hard for normal folk to read.
- Characters from the Royal Family or Thyatian origin are assumed to begin with Thyatian as their primary spoken and written language, and they receive both for free.

### Social Standing Descriptions (from GAZ1)

__Penniless:__ The family is dirt-poor. Most of its members spend all their lives in backbreaking labor. Family members grow up uneducated, working all their lives for someone else’s profit. The character’s “starting gold” represents every coin that the family could scrounge up, save, or steal, and the family is hoping that the character will become wealthy on his adventures and return to help the family. The family owns no property, has practically no possessions and lives in a home of remarkable poverty; family members have ragged clothing, no shoes, no weapon larger than a knife or pitchfork.

__Struggling:__ The family is poor. It may own the property it lives on, but does not make a good living from it. Family members work all their lives to very little gain. The character’s “starting gold” represents several years’ worth of scrimping and saving for the head of the household, and the family is hoping that the character will become wealthy on his adventures and return to help the family. The family lives in an inadequate home; family members have limited clothing and only occasionally own shoes or traveling gear; the household head may own a sword but most members of the family have no weapon other than knives or bows.

__Comfortable:__ The family is not rich but does not suffer from want. It may own the property on which it lives. It makes a decent living with occasional periods of greater prosperity or financial turmoil. The character’s “starting gold” represents the approximate amount the family gives to every youth leaving for the Sharing. The family is hoping that the character will prove to be a worthy person during the years he spends on his own. The family lives in an adequate home; family members have adequate clothes, including footwear, traveling clothes, and (often) one fine outfit for social wear; most members of the family can afford a weapon of choice.

__Wealthy/Untitled:__ The family is wealthy. Its members do not suffer from want. It is likely to own the property on which it lives and other properties besides, and is likely to employ servants. It makes a good living, usually from trade or agriculture or from the earnings of one highly-paid professional in the family.

__Wealthy/Titled:__ This is the same as Wealthy/Untitled, except that the head of the family has a title issued by Duke Stefan or other important figures. He will either bear the title Lord (Lady) or Patriarch (Matriarch). A Lord will be the owner of a large tract of land and leader of the communities thereon, or will be a court lord who performs tasks for Duke Stefan. A Patriarch will be the head of a church or an itinerant (traveling) cleric of great importance within the Church.

__Very Wealthy/Untitled:__ This is the same as Wealthy/Untitled, except that the family is very wealthy-for instance, the family in charge of an important shipping enterprise or of an important community. The DM will need to decide which family the character is from, choosing from the examples given in this Gazetteer. He might, for instance, decide the character belongs to the Torenescu family of Specularum, a very powerful but untitled clan.

__Very Wealthy/Titled:__ This is the same as Wealthy/Titled, except that the family is larger and more important. The head of the clan will be a Baron or a General in the Duke’s service. The DM will need to decide which family the character is from, choosing from appropriate clans. He might, for instance, decide the character belongs to the Kelvin clan of Kelvin or the Vorloi clan of Specularum and Vorloi Village.

__Royal Family:__ The character is a member of the Duke’s own family. The DM will decide precisely what relationship the character has to the Duke. He might decide, for instance, that the character is the Duke’s oldest child, or second-oldest, or that the character is a nephew or niece of the Duke. The character will have recently been Sheared and so cannot depend on the royal family for assistance for quite some time. If the character travels about under his own name, he risks kidnapping, murder and subversion attempts.
